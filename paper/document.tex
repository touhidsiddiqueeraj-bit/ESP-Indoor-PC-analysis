\begin{abstract}
Battery-free indoor nodes must track the photovoltaic maximum power point with the same MCU that harvests the energy. We do not measure energy directly; we measure timing on silicon and derive bounds. The measured single-channel control update takes 71\,$\mu$s on ESP32-S3 at 240\,MHz (239\,$\mu$s on STM32F103 at 72\,MHz, soft-float) and a two-channel P\&O update is $\sim$131\,$\mu$s/15.1\,$\mu$J (STM32 $\sim$304\,$\mu$s) including the extra ADC. At datasheet-typical current this bounds $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ at 8.2--470\,$\mu$J per wake (ESP32; 57$\times$ uncertainty pending rail-current measurement). As a sensitivity, the firmware-vs-PMIC crossover spans seconds to minutes for 1.3--1.7\,$\mu$W quiescent. A simulated SCAPS-1D cell and a simulated P\&O loop ($\sim$70\% at minute cadences) bound harvest; under one office profile and a common boundary (85\% buck-boost + 8.5\,$\mu$W discrete+LDO), a 2\,F supercapacitor bridges the night and a 38\% cell sustains $\sim$1.6--1.7\,k net 100\,mA bursts per day. Timing is measured, harvest and tracking are simulated, and the discrete stage plus current measurement remain required to replace a PMIC.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Battery-free indoor sensing nodes are attractive but power-starved: a few hundred microwatts of photovoltaic (PV) power must be converted into useful work at 200--1000\,lux \cite{ariken2023indoor,mateen2023indoor,freunek2013indoor,derossi2015indoor,mathews2019indoor}, with indoor records now beyond 42\% in wide-bandgap devices \cite{chen2024indoor42} and 44\% \cite{ma2024record,xu2026progress} but office irradiance still 0.05--0.4\,mW/cm$^2$. The conventional path inserts a harvesting PMIC (TI bq25570, e-peas AEM10941, ADI ADP5091) between cell and node for MPPT, charging, protection, regulated output and cold-start \cite{ti_bq25570,ep_aem10941,adi_adp5091}. Those ICs draw 0.4--0.5\,$\mu$A quiescent at 3.3\,V ($\sim$1.3--1.7\,$\mu$W) \cite{vullers2009micropower}.

Indoor cells move slowly, so a perturb every few seconds could in principle cost less than a constantly-on IC. Whether this holds on an ESP32-S3 (240\,MHz, 8\,MB PSRAM) \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet,espressif_s3_trm} depends on three numbers: the perturb timing, the harvestable power, and the deep-sleep floor \cite{murtola2022,brunner2024harvestics}. This paper contributes a revised, review-driven \emph{timing characterization with derived bounds} (not a direct energy measurement): (i) cycle-counted timing of one firmware perturb on silicon on ESP32-S3 @240\,MHz (STM32F103 Blue Pill @72\,MHz soft-float comparison), reported as a lower bound and a harness-inclusive conservative upper bound that includes the wake burst; (ii) a SCAPS-1D indoor sweep (simulated) with published layer parameters and validation against the indoor Shockley--Queisser limit; (iii) a sensitivity analysis of the firmware-vs-PMIC crossover $T_p^*(E_{\mathrm{cycle}})$ against three PMICs under a common boundary; (iv) a simulation-based P\&O tracking study versus perturb period $T_p$ under illumination steps; (v) a 24\,h dispatch with the full hourly ledger and state-of-charge (SoC) enforcement with $\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}(T_p)$ applied; and (vi) measured 4.0\,ms wake latency. We deliberately narrow the claim from ``ditch the PMIC'' to \emph{measured timing and system-level feasibility envelope}, showing what is defensible today and what requires external rail-current measurement and a physical converter.

\section{Methodology}

Three tracks converge on the comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}): measured firmware primitives on silicon (ESP32-S3 @240\,MHz with STM32F103 Blue Pill @72\,MHz soft-float harness), a simulated indoor cell, and an analytic dispatch with a common energy boundary. All refer to the ESP32-S3 as primary platform (STM32 Blue Pill as comparative datapoint), but only timing primitives and wake latency are measured on hardware; harvest is simulated and the converter is modeled. Energy is therefore a \emph{timing-derived bound} ($E=V_{\mathrm{nom}}I_{\mathrm{typ}}T_{\mathrm{meas}}$ with $I_{\mathrm{typ}}$ from datasheets \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet,st_stm32f103}), not a rail measurement---the 57$\times$ $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ uncertainty interval is an explicit admission of unmeasured $I(t)$ during boot.

\subsection{System Boundary and What Firmware Cannot Replace}

A harvesting PMIC is not just an algorithm. It normally provides cold-start circuitry, boost/buck conversion, storage charging, over-voltage/over-current protection, under-voltage lockout, regulated output, reverse blocking and power-path management. Firmware can replace the \emph{algorithm} (P\&O decision and duty update) but not the power stage: PWM from an ESP32-S3 cannot move charge into a supercapacitor without an inductor, switching FET, diode/capacitor, shunt/current-sense amplifier and a regulator from the 3.0--4.2\,V capacitor to the 3.3\,V rail. We therefore compare three architectures under one boundary $P_{\mathrm{harvest}}\rightarrow E_{\mathrm{stored}}$: (a) \emph{Discrete + firmware}: PV $\rightarrow$ buck-boost (85\% efficiency) $\rightarrow$ 2\,F super-cap (3.0--4.2\,V, $E_{\max}=0.5C(V_{\max}^2-V_{\min}^2)=8.64$\,J $\approx$8.6\,J) $\rightarrow$ LDO to 3.3\,V; (b) \emph{PMIC}: PV $\rightarrow$ integrated harvesting IC $\rightarrow$ storage $\rightarrow$ system rail; (c) baselines (fixed-ratio, direct) where relevant. Discrete sense ($\sim$0.3\,$\mu$W) and LDO quiescent ($\sim$2.5\,$\mu$A $\rightarrow$ 8.5\,$\mu$W, $P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}$) are added to the firmware side so the comparison is not artificially favorable to firmware.

\subsection{Firmware Hub: Two-Level Control (P\&O $\rightarrow$ PID)}

P\&O must observe \emph{power}, not voltage alone: $P=V_{pv}I_{pv}$ requires two ADC channels (e.g.\ $V_{pv}$ on ADC1\_CH0 via the 0--2.5\,V range at 11\,dB, $I_{pv}$ via a 0.1\,$\Omega$ shunt/INA219 on ADC1\_CH1). The outer loop generates a reference every $T_p$:
{\footnotesize
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_k &= V_k I_k, \\
\text{if } P_k > P_{k-1}&\text{ keep else flip},\\
V_{\mathrm{ref},k} &= V_{\mathrm{ref},k-1} + s_k\Delta V,\ s_k\in\{+1,-1\},\ \Delta V=20\,\mathrm{mV}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}}

The inner PID tracks that reference to a duty command every $T_s$ ($\sim$62\,$\mu$s single-read, $\sim$6.8\,ms at 64$\times$ oversampling). Velocity form (no windup accumulation):

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e &= V_{\mathrm{meas}}-V_{\mathrm{ref}},\\
\Delta u &= K_p(e-e_1)+K_iT_s e+\tfrac{K_d}{T_s}(e-2e_1+e_2),\\
d &\leftarrow \mathrm{clamp}(d+\Delta u, 0.05, 0.95),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pid}
\end{equation}
with $K_p$=0.05, $K_i$=100, $K_d$=1e-5 at 100\,kHz switching; a retuned bandwidth-matched set $K_p$=0.02, $K_i$=0.5, $K_d$=0 is used for $>$1\,ms periods. An EMA $\alpha$=0.08 filters the sample before the PID, and orthogonal dither delays decorrelate reads from switching ripple. A three-state scheduler (fast single-read, slow oversampled, $\pm$1-LSB dither) with hysteresis and debounce mitigates quantization limit cycles (pseudocode in supplementary material).

On the present bench the PV and converter are absent: GPIO2 (LEDC, 100\,kHz) is jumper-shorted to GPIO1 (ADC1\_CH0), so the sensed ``output'' is $d\cdot V_{\mathrm{logic}}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:step}). This measures \emph{timing} (the review's ``loopback timing test''), not power transfer. Sec.~\ref{sec:mpptsim} supplies the missing $P$-based evaluation in simulation; a future bench must break the jumper, attach the discrete stage and two ADC channels, and run the same control loop (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}) as mode~M.

\subsection{On-Silicon Measurement Protocol (Honest Timing-Derived Bounds)}

Wall time per primitive comes from the cycle counter (\texttt{ccount}) while busy-looping at the buck-boost MPP proxy. Deep-sleep is characterized with RTC-persistent counters (\texttt{RTC\_DATA\_ATTR} round count, \texttt{esp\_timer} wake stamp), timer-wake, and CSV logging after a 50\,ms USB-CDC settle. Five consecutive 3\,s wakes constitute one campaign. Only time $T$ is measured on this bench; $V$ and $I$ are not---energy is therefore derived, not measured.

A node cannot observe its own rail current without an external meter. We therefore report timing-derived energy \emph{bounds} using datasheet-typical active current ($I_{\mathrm{act}}$=35\,mA ESP32-S3 \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet}, 36\,mA STM32F103 \cite{st_stm32f103} at 3.3\,V; $I_q$ varies with mode, temperature and regulator, so these are typ.~bounds pending rail-current measurement):
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{step}} = V I_{\mathrm{act}} T_{\mathrm{step}} = 3.3\,\mathrm{V}\times35\,\mathrm{mA}\times71\,\mu\mathrm{s}=8.2\,\mu\mathrm{J}
\end{equation}
(lower bound: marginal cost of the single-channel control-update sequence; two-channel P\&O $\approx$15.1\,$\mu$J) and a \emph{conservative harness-inclusive upper bound} that assumes the full 4.0\,ms firmware-visible burst at $I_{\mathrm{act}}$:
$E_{\mathrm{wake,worst}} = V I_{\mathrm{act}} T_{\mathrm{wake}} \approx 462\,\mu\mathrm{J}$, so $E_{\mathrm{cycle,upper}} \approx 470\,\mu\mathrm{J}$. Operational definition: $T_{\mathrm{wake}}$=4.0\,ms is the interval from first firmware-visible \texttt{esp\_timer} stamp (setup entry) through the 71\,$\mu$s control update to deep-sleep entry; it excludes the 0.28\,s host-side excess per 3.28\,s period (3.0\,s programmed + ROM boot + USB-CDC re-enumeration before the stamp, plus 50\,ms settle). A deployed node without a USB-serial bridge (CP210x/CH340) will wake faster and the true $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ sits below this ceiling by an unquantified amount. Hence $8.2\,\mu\mathrm{J}\le E_{\mathrm{cycle}}\le 470\,\mu\mathrm{J}$ (57$\times$ uncertainty interval) is an explicit admission of unmeasured $I(t)$ during boot, not a tight measurement---the true crossover satisfies the corresponding $T_p^*$ sensitivity band (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}). An external Joulescope/Otii/shunt-scope rail-current trace from reset through return-to-sleep is required to collapse this band to a point measurement.

\subsection{Indoor Cell Model (Simulated, Not Measured)}
\label{sec:scaps}

Harvest is simulated in SCAPS-1D \cite{burgelman2000} on \texttt{perovskite-mapi.def} (FTO/TiO$_2$/MAPbI$_3$/Spiro/Au, n-i-p, coefficients $A$=1e7\,/m/$\sqrt{\mathrm{eV}}$) under the bundled ``white 400--800\,nm'' spectrum at 0.05\,mW/cm$^2$ ($\sim$400--500\,lux; the lux$\rightarrow$irradiance and area coupling is stated explicitly in Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison} Eq.~\ref{eq:lux2harv}). The Shockley--Queisser limit \cite{shockley1961detailed,freunek2013indoor} bounds any single-junction cell. Bandgap is swept 1.6--2.1\,eV with conduction-band alignment $\chi=3.9+0.5(E_g-1.55)$\,eV. Parameters are in Table~\ref{tab:scapsparams}; six JV curves are extracted. We validate against the indoor detailed-balance envelope and literature 18--33\% devices in this regime---specifically 18\% carbon-electrode PSC \cite{makming2025cnt}, 23\% indoor-tuned FAPbI$_3$ \cite{marrugat2025tuning}, 27\% engineered transport layers \cite{datt2024engineered}, and 33\% methylammonium-free wide-bandgap \cite{shi2025enabling}---with recent 42\% wide-bandgap results \cite{chen2024indoor42,ma2024record} marking the upper envelope, and explain the spectrum dependence of the optimum.

\subsection{Comparison and Dispatch Models}
\label{sec:comparison}

PMICs enter via datasheet quiescent at the stated terminal (tax referred to the 3.3\,V rail for apples-to-apples; bq25570 $I_q$=488\,nA at $V_{\mathrm{STOR}}$=2.1\,V $\rightarrow$ 1.02\,$\mu$W at 2.1\,V, 1.61\,$\mu$W referred to 3.3\,V): bq25570 488\,nA ($V_{\mathrm{STOR}}$=2.1\,V), AEM10941 400\,nA ($V_{\mathrm{BATT}}$=3\,V, LDOs off), ADP5091 510\,nA (SYS) \cite{ti_bq25570,ep_aem10941,adi_adp5091}, consistent with indoor models \cite{brunner2024harvestics}. The firmware average tax is $P_{\mathrm{fw}}(T_p)=E_{\mathrm{cycle}}/T_p + P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}$, where $P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}\approx8.5\,\mu$W (sense+LDO) and $\eta_{\mathrm{conv}}$=0.85. The sensitivity $T_p^*=E_{\mathrm{cycle}}/P_q$ below is the $E/T_p$ vs.\ $P_q$ comparison \emph{before} adding $P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}$; adding it makes $P_{\mathrm{fw}}>P_q$ on quiescent alone (8.5\,$\mu$W $>$ 1.3--1.7\,$\mu$W)---firmware can only win once total system cost is workload-weighted (Sec.~\ref{sec:crossover}).

Harvest coupling (the number that converts SCAPS irradiance to dispatch power) is stated explicitly:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{harv}}(L)=\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}(T_p)\,J_{sc}V_{mp}A(1-\ell)L/L_0
\label{eq:lux2harv}
\end{equation}
with bundled spectrum $L_0$=1000\,lux $\equiv$ 0.10\,mW/cm$^2$ (0.05\,mW/cm$^2$ $\equiv$ $\sim$500\,lux for the white 400--800\,nm source used in SCAPS), cell area $A$=10\,cm$^2$, interconnect/shading loss $\ell$=0.15, and $J_{sc}V_{mp}$ from the SCAPS 1.6\,eV $JV$ at that irradiance; this scales the simulated 1.6\,eV cell to 0.55\,mW at 1000\,lux for dispatch (DSC 38\% $\rightarrow$ 1.87\,mW and a-Si $\rightarrow$ 1.2\,mW literature references use the same $A,\ell,L_0$).

Dispatch is an hourly ledger over $H$=96 steps. A single office profile drives every number:
\begin{equation}
L(t)=\begin{cases}
1000\,\mathrm{lux} & 8\le t<19\\
300 & 6\le t<8 \text{ or } 19\le t<20\\
50 & \text{otherwise (night leak)}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
so night is 13\,h at 50\,lux ($\ne$ the 5\,h sub-interval). With SoC $E[t]$,
{\footnotesize
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E[t+1] = \mathrm{clamp}_{0}^{E_{\max}}\!\bigl(E[t] &+ \eta_{\mathrm{sto}}\eta_{\mathrm{conv}}\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}(T_p)P_{pv}[L]\Delta t \\
&- (P_{\mathrm{sleep}}+P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}})\Delta t \\
&- E_{\mathrm{serve}}[t] - E_{\mathrm{mppt}}(T_p)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dispatch}
\end{equation}}
where $P_{\mathrm{sleep}}=VI_{\mathrm{sleep}}$, $E_{\mathrm{serve}}$=0.033\,J per 100\,mA/100\,ms burst, $\eta_{\mathrm{sto}}$=0.95, $E_{\max}\approx8.6$\,J ($0.5C(V_{\max}^2-V_{\min}^2)$ with $C$=2\,F, 3.0--4.2\,V via LDO to 3.3\,V; regulator loss and quiescent are in $P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}$). A feasible schedule keeps $0\le E[t]\le E_{\max}$ at all steps over $\ge$24\,h. Night bridging is reported for both the 13\,h full night (3.24\,J) and the 5\,h worst-case dark gap (1.25\,J) at 21\,$\mu$A. An hourly table is published in supplementary data; the paper excerpts the integrated budget.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Perturb-Step Timing on Silicon (Measured) and Derived Lower Bound}
\label{sec:step}

Fig.~\ref{fig:step} and Table~\ref{tab:timing} decompose one perturb: ADC 60.0\,$\mu$s (84.5\%), PID 6.78\,$\mu$s, PWM 4.2\,$\mu$s, total $T_{\mathrm{step}}$=70.98\,$\mu$s. Only $T$ is measured; timing-derived $E_{\mathrm{step}}$=8.2\,$\mu$J at 3.3\,V/35\,mA (datasheet-typical \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet}). At $T_p$=1\,s this is 8.2\,$\mu$W, at 60\,s 0.14\,$\mu$W, at 1000\,s 0.008\,$\mu$W. This is the correct \emph{marginal} cost of the measured single-channel control-update sequence for the loopback bench, not the cost of waking the chip. An implementable two-channel P\&O needs $P=V_{pv}I_{pv}$ $\rightarrow$ $T_{\mathrm{step,P\&O}}\approx$131\,$\mu$s (2$\times$ADC+PID+PWM) and $E_{\mathrm{step,P\&O}}\approx$15.1\,$\mu$J, 1.85$\times$ the table value; the 8.2\,$\mu$J is therefore also a lower bound on the P\&O step.

\subsection{Deep-Sleep Wake Overhead (Measured $T$, Bounded $E$)}
\label{sec:wake}

Five consecutive 3\,s timer wakes show 4.0\,ms firmware-visible bursts every 3.28\,s (Fig.~\ref{fig:sleepwave}); the burst is defined as firmware entry (\texttt{esp\_timer} stamp) through the 71\,$\mu$s single-channel update to sleep entry, after a 50\,ms USB-CDC settle. The 0.28\,s excess (3.28\,s\,$-$\,3.00\,s) is ROM boot + USB re-enumeration before that stamp plus host-side delay, and is \emph{not} charged at $I_{\mathrm{act}}$ in the upper bound. If the 4\,ms burst ran at the deep-sleep floor ($\sim$33\,$\mu$A) it would add 0.44\,$\mu$J (5\% of $E_{\mathrm{step}}$); at datasheet-typical 35\,mA \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet} it would add 462\,$\mu$J (56$\times$ $E_{\mathrm{step}}$). The firmware returns to deep sleep immediately after the control update, but the boot interval before it is not measured on the rail---its current waveform requires external current measurement. We therefore quote a timing-derived ESP32 $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}\in[8.2,470]\,\mu$J per wake where the upper bound is explicitly harness-inclusive. STM32F103 at 72\,MHz (Blue Pill, soft-float, \texttt{stm32duino} via USART1/CH341) \cite{st_stm32f103} measures 64.3\,$\mu$s ADC, 135.9\,$\mu$s float PID and 25\,$\mu$s PWM for a 239\,$\mu$s single-channel step (two-channel P\&O $\sim$304\,$\mu$s); at 36\,mA this is $E_{\mathrm{step}}$=28.4\,$\mu$J (36.1\,$\mu$J two-channel) and harness-inclusive $E_{\mathrm{cycle,upper}}\approx$504\,$\mu$J, i.e.\ 3.4$\times$ the ESP32 marginal cost where the PID dominates soft-float. A fixed-point \texttt{int32} PID (ESP32 1.33\,$\mu$s) would close most of that gap.

\subsection{Indoor Harvest Bound (Simulated)}
\label{sec:harvest}

 Fig.~\ref{fig:scaps} shows the simulated JV families. $V_{oc}$ rises 0.897\,V (1.6\,eV) to 1.48\,V (2.1\,eV), $J_{sc}$ falls 0.0182 to 0.0075\,mA/cm$^2$, peak PCE 26.1\% at 1.6\,eV (Fig.~\ref{fig:scapsvalid}). The simulated window sits inside the ESP32-S3 0--2.5\,V ADC range at 11\,dB, so no divider is needed for a single-junction perovskite cell. For dispatch we scale the 1.6\,eV device to 0.55\,mW at 1000\,lux ($\sim$10\,cm$^2$ plus interconnect loss) alongside literature DSC 1.87\,mW (38\%) and a-Si 1.2\,mW references.

The bandgap trade-off is asymmetric: $E_g$ 1.6$\rightarrow$2.1\,eV lifts $V_{oc}$ by 0.58\,V but cuts $J_{sc}$ by 59\%; under the broad white spectrum the narrow-gap device wins on power despite lower voltage. Under a warm LED spectrum the optimum shifts toward $\sim$1.9\,eV \cite{xu2026progress,ma2024record}; our $A$=1e7 coefficient model correctly tracks Eg but the absolute optimum is spectrum-dependent.

\subsection{Control-Tax Crossover as Sensitivity to Unmeasured $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$}
\label{sec:crossover}

Fig.~\ref{fig:overhead} and Table~\ref{tab:ic} give the revised comparison as a \emph{sensitivity analysis}, not a point result. Because $T_p^*=E_{\mathrm{cycle}}/P_q$, any crossover is linear in the unmeasured $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$. Using only the timing-derived lower bound $E_{\mathrm{step}}$ reproduces the original 4.9--6.2\,s crossovers; using the harness-inclusive conservative $E_{\mathrm{cycle,upper}}$ pushes them to 279--356\,s (STM32: 16.9--21.5\,s vs.\ 299--381\,s). Adding the discrete-stage floor $P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}\approx8.5\,\mu$W (sense\,+\,LDO) to the firmware side makes $P_{\mathrm{fw}}(T_p)=E_{\mathrm{cycle}}/T_p+P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}$ strictly $>$1.3--1.7\,$\mu$W on quiescent alone---there is no quiescent-power crossover under that floor, as Fig.~\ref{fig:overhead} shows with a second band (dashed). Firmware can only compete via BOM/configurability or workload-level duty, not lower static power; the ``somewhere between 5\,s and 5\,min'' interval is thus the sensitivity to unmeasured wake energy, not a result, and a rail-current measurement is required to select the true $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ within the 57$\times$ uncertainty interval.

N_{\mathrm{net}}(T_p) = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}(T_p)E_{\mathrm{harv}} - E_{\mathrm{sleep}} - P_{\mathrm{discrete,q}}T_{24}}{E_{\mathrm{serve}}}

\section{Discussion}

The original ``ditch the PMIC'' title overstated the measurement. The PMIC's cold-start difficulty at $<$0.5\,mA \cite{brunner2024harvestics} does not disappear---it is transferred to the discrete stage and MCU brown-out domain. A firmware hub has no \emph{firmware} cold-start, but it cannot run until the capacitor has bootstrapped a regulated 3.3\,V rail; the discrete stage must be self-starting (e.g.\ diode-OR or native-start FET) and the MCU needs $>$2.8\,V with sufficient input current at the installed lux. Measuring startup from 0\,V, 0.5\,V, 1.5\,V and 3.0\,V at 50--1000\,lux is required before any ``no cold-start'' claim.

Treating SCAPS as a measurement was also inappropriate: abstract and results now state ``simulated.'' The bandgap trade-off under the broad white spectrum is validated against the indoor detailed-balance envelope (Fig.~\ref{fig:scapsvalid}) and the spectrum dependence is explicit; a deployed validation would replace the simulated harvest with a lamp-specific measured JV curve, vary defect density ($N_t$=1e13--1e17\,/cm$^3$, i.e.\ 1e19--1e23\,/m$^3$), and publish thickness/mobility/recombination settings (Table~\ref{tab:scapsparams}). Bandgap-universal passivation \cite{lin2024bandgap}, pyridine passivation \cite{han2025pyridine} and engineered transport layers \cite{datt2024engineered} would only widen margin if validated.

Three limitations bound the conclusions. First, the perturb timing is measured but harvest and tracking are simulated; the P\&O $\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}(T_p)$ table and SoC curves are model outputs, not bench MPPT demonstrations. Second, the sleep floor is literature/literature-derived and assumed constant; real leakage varies with temperature, capacitor SoC and peripherals, and the true rail current including boot requires external current measurement (the $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ band is timing-derived, not measured). Third, dispatch assumes predictable occupancy and ignores aging/dust \cite{asuo2026stability}; a deployed loop would need periodic $V_{oc}$ recalibration, which firmware supports naturally and a fixed-ratio PMIC does not. The fixed open-circuit-voltage (FOCV) approximation, often used for ultra-low-power MPPT, is itself occupancy- and temperature-sensitive indoors \cite{azlor2025focv} and motivates the P\&O approach studied in Sec.~\ref{sec:mpptsim}.

Within these bounds the sensitivity is modest: the 1.32--1.68\,$\mu$W PMIC taxes cluster, so 1\,$\mu$A error shifts $T_p^*$ by seconds at the lower bound (and $\sim$100\,s at the upper bound). The dispatch is sleep-floor dominated at $\ge$5\,min cadence (Table~\ref{tab:cadence}); board leakage, not the perturb period, is the first line to optimize, as the reviewer observed.

\section{Conclusion}

Firmware MPPT control on an ESP32-S3 is \emph{in the range of} 71\,$\mu$s per single-channel update (131\,$\mu$s two-channel P\&O); timing-derived energy is 8.2\,$\mu$J (15.1\,$\mu$J two-channel P\&O, datasheet-typical 35\,mA \cite{espressif_s3_datasheet}) with a harness-inclusive per-wake upper bound of $\sim$470\,$\mu$J ($\sim$477\,$\mu$J two-channel P\&O) pending rail-current measurement. That puts the crossover sensitivity at $\sim$5\,s (9\,s two-channel P\&O) lower bound to $\sim$5\,min upper bound---a sensitivity to unmeasured $E_{\mathrm{cycle}}$, not a point result, and no quiescent-power crossover exists once the 8.5\,$\mu$W discrete-stage floor is included. A simulated SCAPS-1D perovskite sweep bounds indoor harvest (26.1\% at 1.6\,eV, \emph{simulated}, scaled via Eq.~\ref{eq:lux2harv}); P\&O on that curve shows 93\% at 5\,s and $\sim$71\% at $\ge$60\,s. Under a consistent office profile and common boundary (85\% buck-boost + 8.5\,$\mu$W LDO), 38\% DSC sustains $\sim$1730 net bursts/day at 100\% $\eta_{\mathrm{MPPT}}$ ($\sim$1600 at 93\% $T_p$=5\,s, Eq.~\ref{eq:nnet} reconciled with Tab.~\ref{tab:ledger} and Fig.~\ref{fig:dispatch}), and the 8.64\,J super-cap bridges the 13\,h night with margin. Design guidance: keep a PMIC for sub-10\,s tracking or sub-mV regulation; consider firmware for $\ge$30--60\,s sensing where the sleep floor dominates and the saving is the PMIC IC itself ($\sim$\$1--3) with the discrete stage still required ($\sim$15--25\,mm$^2$); size the sleep floor first; provide the discrete stage and rail measurement before claiming replacement. Future work is a PV--converter--storage bench with $V_{pv}\times I_{pv}$ sensing, cold-start sweeps, and side-by-side PMIC evaluation.
